What it does

Most feeds keep you alert. This one follows a calm arc in seven stages, built from research on color, sound and breathing.

Color that softens

Each video has its own color. Saturation falls through the session and the palette moves from warm coral and gold to cool teal, blue and lavender. Two videos in a row never share a hue family. At night, a sideways swipe turns the palette warm and dim, with no blue.

Notes that descend

Each video sounds one note from a consonant five-note scale. Pitch drifts lower, the sound softens by about 14 dB and the session ends on a low, settled home note.

A rhythm you can breathe to

Stay on a video and its note starts to breathe with you. Each breath begins at a normal resting pace and lengthens step by step toward six breaths a minute.

How to use it

No settings to learn. Open it before bed or whenever your mind is racing.

  1. Open it

    The feed starts as soon as the app opens, and the sound starts with your first touch. A session lasts about 15 minutes, or around 120 short videos at a quick pace.

  2. Swipe as usual

    Swipe up for the next video, exactly like any feed. Each one swaps in a new color and note.

  3. Linger on what feels good

    Stay on a video and its note keeps breathing. Your screen dims a little as the breaths grow longer.

  4. Drift off

    Leave it for three minutes and the app rests. The screen can sleep and any touch wakes it.

Day and night

At night, swipe right. The colors turn warm and dim, with no blue, and grow a little darker with every video. Swipe left to bring the day colors back. Faint arrows at the edges show the way until you have used them, a short note names the mode, and the app keeps your choice for next time. The sound stays the same.

Try it on the phone above: swipe sideways, press ← or →, or tap Night.

It learns what calms you

A small learning model watches one signal: how long you stay. Every session it gets a little better at showing you the colors and notes you linger on, without ever leaving the calm arc.

1

It notices lingers

A video counts as a linger when you stay at least 1.5 times longer than on the videos around it, so later, slower parts of a session are not mistaken for favorites.

2

It keeps score

Every remembered video carries a running score of lingers against skips. The model samples from those scores (Thompson sampling) to decide what comes back.

3

It explores, too

Favorites fill at most a quarter of each stage. The rest is fresh, and colors and notes you liked get a gentle boost in new draws.

4

It forgets slowly

Scores fade by 15% each session, so last month's favorite makes room for tonight's mood.
